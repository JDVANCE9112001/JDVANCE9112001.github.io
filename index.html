01 About

I like problems where the answer has to survive contact with the real world — a bracket that has to hold a motor, a script that has to run on someone else's laptop, an agent that has to answer from a document instead of from its imagination.

My background is mechanical: CAD, standard parts, assembly, and the endless small decisions that decide whether a chassis is rigid or just looks rigid. Lately I have been moving toward the software side, because the most interesting robotics problems are the ones where the machine has to perceive, decide, and then act.

02 What I'm doing now

Building with agents

Taking a course on building agentic AI products. The part I find hardest — and most interesting — is the loop: the model proposes an action, a harness runs it, the result comes back as context, and the model decides what to do next.

Robot chassis work

Working through the mechanical training material for our RoboMaster team: standard parts, frame layout, and checking that every dimension on the drawing matches the part in the box.

Learning Git properly

Committing small changes, reading my own history, pushing to GitHub, then editing a file on the website and pulling it back. Still surprised by how much of the workflow is bookkeeping rather than typing.

Reading list

A short stack: a Python tutorial, an operating-systems reader, and whatever the instructors hand out. I keep notes in Markdown so they stay searchable.
